Network Connections The app connects to other computers only because of torrents you add, and only while those torrents are active: Peers: other people sharing the same torrent. Your IP address is visible to them, as it is for any torrent client, and the parts you download are uploaded to them while you download. That is how BitTorrent works. Trackers listed in the torrent, plus the distributed hash table (DHT), peer exchange and local peer discovery when enabled in Settings. These help find peers. Web addresses you enter or share: when you add a direct HTTPS link to a .torrent file, the app downloads that file from that server. For your safety it refuses links to local or private network addresses. A proxy server you set up (optional). Its connection test contacts example.com through your proxy. Your router, when UPnP or NAT-PMP port mapping is enabled. Firebase Crashlytics (Google), for crash reports, unless you turned them off. Crash Reports If the app crashes, it sends a report through Firebase Crashlytics, a Google service, so the problem can be found and fixed. A report contains where in the app's code the crash happened, the app and Android versions, the device model, some device state such as free memory and storage, and random installation IDs. Error messages are removed before sending, so the names of your torrents and files, their paths, magnet links, tracker addresses and peers' addresses are never included. While reports are on, Crashlytics also notes when the app starts, so the share of sessions without a crash can be counted. Reports are kept for up to 90 days. The app does not read the Android advertising ID. Reports are on unless you turn them off in Settings > About > Send crash reports. Turning them off also deletes the reports not sent yet, and crashes recorded while they are off are never sent. Firebase Crashlytics: https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy Google: https://policies.google.com/privacy Data Stored on Your Device The torrents you add, their files and download progress (including resume data that lets downloads continue after interruptions), and a history of added, completed, removed and exported torrents and of storage operations, stay on your device. So do the names and tags you give torrents, and your settings. The optional proxy password is encrypted with a key held in Android's keystore. Logs written to the system log for troubleshooting never contain magnet links, tracker addresses, passwords or file paths. All of this is deleted when you remove the torrents, clear the app's data or uninstall the app. Settings exports are files you create and control; they never include the proxy password. Files on Your Device Downloads are written to the app's own storage, and the app asks for no storage permission. When you export finished downloads, they are copied to your Downloads folder or to a folder you choose with the system file picker. When you save a torrent's .torrent file, it goes where you choose. Your downloads also appear, read-only, in the system Files app and in file pickers, so you can browse them or open them in another app you choose. Nothing can be changed or deleted from there, and no other app can reach them unless you pick a file for it. Clipboard "Copy magnet link" puts the torrent's magnet link on the clipboard. A private torrent's link contains your personal tracker address (passkey); the app warns you before copying it and marks it as sensitive, so Android hides it from clipboard previews. Backups If Android backup is on, Android may include the app's settings in your device backup to your Google account, and copy them to a new phone. This is managed by Android and Google under your backup settings and Google's privacy policy; I have no access to it. Your torrents, downloaded files and history are never part of it, and the proxy password can't be read from a backup, because the key that encrypts it never leaves the device. Permissions TorrentDownloader Asks For Internet and network state: to connect to peers and trackers, react to network changes and send crash reports. Notifications: to show download progress and completion. This is optional; downloads run without it. Foreground service (data sync) and user-initiated jobs: to keep downloads running when you leave the app, as Android requires. Receive boot completed: to offer to continue unfinished downloads after a restart, when you turned that on. Wake lock: to keep the processor awake while a transfer is actively running. Local network access (Android 17, optional): for local peer discovery, port mapping and peers on your own network. It is asked for only from Settings. Sharing I do not sell, rent or share your data.
